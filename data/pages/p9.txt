Primary open-angle glaucoma and ocular hypertension
Primary open-angle glaucoma (POAG) is a chronic, progressive optic neuropathy with characteristic
optic disc cupping and visual field loss, in an eye with an open angle and no secondary cause. It is usually
painless and found at a routine eye test. Risk factors include raised IOP, older age, black African or
Caribbean ethnicity, a family history, myopia and a thin central cornea.

Ocular hypertension (OHT) means an IOP above 21 mmHg with a healthy optic disc and visual field. In the
UK, NICE guidance (NG81, updated 2022) recommends offering treatment when the IOP is 24 mmHg or
more and the person is at risk of visual impairment in their lifetime. For both OHT and POAG, 360-degree
selective laser trabeculoplasty (SLT) is now offered first, with a generic prostaglandin analogue as the
usual first-choice medicine if SLT is unsuitable or not wanted. This change followed the LiGHT trial.

Normal-tension glaucoma describes glaucomatous damage with IOP that is always within the normal
range. Vascular factors such as low blood pressure at night, migraine and Raynaud's phenomenon may
play a role.
