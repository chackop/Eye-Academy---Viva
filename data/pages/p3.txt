    Primary angle closure suspect (PACS): ITC for at least 180 degrees, with normal IOP, no PAS and no
    glaucomatous optic neuropathy.
    Primary angle closure (PAC): ITC with raised IOP and/or PAS, but a healthy optic disc and visual field.
    Other signs of previous closure, such as iris whorling or sectoral atrophy, glaukomflecken or excess
    trabecular pigment, support the diagnosis.
    Primary angle closure glaucoma (PACG): angle closure with glaucomatous optic neuropathy.
    Acute primary angle closure (APAC): a sudden symptomatic rise in IOP caused by angle closure,
    called an acute angle-closure crisis in the American guidance. It can occur at any point along this
    spectrum.
    Subacute (intermittent) angle closure: milder, spontaneously resolving episodes, often relieved by
    bright light or sleep, which both constrict the pupil.

Risk factors
    Hypermetropia and short axial length (nanophthalmos, below 20 mm, carries particular risk)
    Older age, as the lens thickens and moves forward
    Female sex
    East Asian or Inuit ethnicity
    A family history of angle closure
    A shallow anterior chamber and a thick or anteriorly placed lens

Mechanisms
Mechanisms are grouped by the level at which they act, from the iris backwards. More than one is often
present (mixed mechanism).

    Pupil block. Contact between the iris and lens slows the flow of aqueous through the pupil. Pressure
    builds behind the iris, which bows forward (iris bombé) and closes the angle. This is the most
    common mechanism and is relieved by a peripheral iridotomy.
    Plateau iris and a thick peripheral iris roll. The ciliary processes sit anteriorly and push the
    peripheral iris into the angle, even though the central chamber may look reasonably deep. Non-pupil-
    block mechanisms are relatively more important in East Asian patients. See the plateau iris section
    below.
    Lens factor. A thick or forward lens crowds the anterior segment. Removing the lens deepens the
    chamber and opens the angle. Closure that is mainly lens-driven (phacomorphic or a subluxed lens)
    is usually classed as secondary.


Acute primary angle closure
Symptoms
    Sudden, severe, aching pain in one eye, often with a frontal headache on the same side. About 5% of
    attacks affect both eyes at once.
    Blurred vision and coloured haloes around lights, caused by corneal oedema
