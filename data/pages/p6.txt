Recheck the IOP every 30 to 60 minutes. Indenting the central cornea with a gonioscopy lens or a squint
hook can push aqueous into the angle and help break the attack. Topical glycerol can clear corneal
epithelial oedema first, which improves the view and reduces the risk of abrasion.

If the IOP does not fall
    Review the diagnosis, for example aqueous misdirection or a secondary cause
    Early laser iridotomy, after clearing the cornea with topical glycerol
    Laser peripheral iridoplasty to pull the peripheral iris away from the angle, particularly if the cornea is
    too cloudy for an iridotomy or systemic carbonic anhydrase inhibitors must be avoided
    Anterior chamber paracentesis as a temporary measure. It works quickly but the effect is short-lived
    and there is a small risk of damage to the lens.
    Surgical peripheral iridectomy, early lens extraction (with or without goniosynechialysis),
    trabeculectomy or cyclodiode, ideally in a specialist glaucoma unit

Definitive treatment
    Laser peripheral iridotomy (LPI) in the affected eye once the cornea has cleared, usually within a
    few days. Continue topical steroid for at least a week.
    Prophylactic LPI in the fellow eye, because without treatment around 40 to 50% of fellow eyes have
    an attack within 5 to 10 years (higher figures are reported in some series). Long-term pilocarpine is
    not an adequate alternative.
    Lens extraction is an alternative or addition to LPI, particularly when the lens is thick or cataractous,
    and there should be a low threshold for it after an attack. In the EAGLE trial, clear lens extraction in
    people aged 50 or over with PAC (IOP 30 mmHg or more) or PACG gave better pressure control and
    quality of life than LPI, and was cost-effective. EAGLE did not study eyes during or straight after an
    acute attack.

Follow-up
Once the eye has settled, repeat gonioscopy (including the affected eye, which could not be assessed
properly during the attack), examine both optic discs and arrange visual field testing. Check that the
iridotomies are patent. Some eyes develop chronically raised IOP from PAS or trabecular damage and
need long-term medical or surgical treatment. Any trigger drug should be stopped and the patient
warned about others.
