Laser and surgery in glaucoma
    Selective laser trabeculoplasty (SLT) treats the trabecular meshwork to increase outflow in open-
    angle glaucoma and ocular hypertension. It can be repeated.
    Laser peripheral iridotomy relieves pupil block in angle closure (see the iridotomy section).
    Laser iridoplasty contracts the peripheral iris to pull it away from the angle, mainly for plateau iris or
    an attack that does not respond to medicine.
    Cyclodiode laser reduces aqueous production by treating the ciliary body. Conventional
    continuous-wave treatment is used for eyes with a poor visual outlook or after other treatments have
    failed; micropulse treatment is sometimes used earlier.
    Trabeculectomy creates a guarded channel from the anterior chamber to a bleb under the
    conjunctiva. Antifibrotic agents such as mitomycin C or 5-fluorouracil are used to reduce scarring. In
    angle closure eyes the risk of aqueous misdirection is higher.
    Tube shunts (such as Baerveldt, Ahmed or PAUL) drain aqueous to a plate placed further back on the
    eye, often after failed trabeculectomy or in neovascular and uveitic glaucoma.
    Minimally invasive glaucoma surgery (MIGS) uses small devices, often placed at the same time as
    cataract surgery, for mild to moderate glaucoma.
    Lens extraction deepens the anterior chamber and is an effective treatment for many people with
    angle closure (see EAGLE above).
