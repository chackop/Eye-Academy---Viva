Assessing the drainage angle
Van Herick grading
With a thin, bright slit beam offset about 60 degrees from the microscope and placed at the temporal
limbus, compare the depth of the most peripheral anterior chamber with the thickness of the cornea.

  Grade       Peripheral chamber depth                       Interpretation


  4           Equal to or greater than corneal thickness     Wide open

  3           Between a quarter and a half                   Closure unlikely

  2           A quarter                                      Needs gonioscopy

  1           Less than a quarter                            Angle usually dangerously narrow on gonioscopy


Van Herick is a screening test only. It can overestimate angle width, particularly in plateau iris
configuration, so grades 1 and 2 (and any clinical suspicion) should be confirmed with gonioscopy.

Gonioscopy
Gonioscopy is the reference standard for seeing the angle structures directly. Use a dark room and a
short, thin slit beam that avoids the pupil, because light constricts the pupil and can open an angle that
would be closed in the dark (this happens in about a third of appositionally closed angles). From
posterior to anterior the visible structures are the ciliary body band, scleral spur, trabecular meshwork
and Schwalbe's line. The angle is usually narrowest superiorly.

      Indentation (dynamic) gonioscopy with a four-mirror lens pushes aqueous into the angle. If the iris
      falls back, the closure is appositional; if it stays stuck to the meshwork, there are peripheral anterior
      synechiae (PAS).
      Iridotrabecular contact (ITC) means the peripheral iris touches the posterior (pigmented)
      trabecular meshwork. ITC for at least 180 degrees is the threshold used by the American Academy of
      Ophthalmology and in recent trials. The original international (ISGEO) definition, still used in Kanski,
      requires three or more quadrants (270 degrees).
      Shaffer grading records the angle width in each quadrant: grade 4 (35 to 45 degrees, ciliary body
      visible), grade 3 (scleral spur visible), grade 2 (trabecular meshwork but not scleral spur visible),
      grade 1 (only Schwalbe's line and perhaps the top of the meshwork visible), slit, and grade 0 (closed).

Anterior segment OCT and ultrasound biomicroscopy (UBM) give images of the angle and complement
gonioscopy, but they do not replace it. UBM is better for plateau iris and other ciliary body causes,
because it can see behind the iris.
